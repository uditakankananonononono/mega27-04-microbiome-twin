I would treat the failed source-transfer diagnostics as a signal that the original “digital twin” framing is probably too ambitious unless you can define one narrow biological prediction task with a real held-out generalization target. A microbiome twin that “simulates a person” is difficult to validate because most public datasets lack intervention ground truth, absolute abundance, dense longitudinal sampling, and synchronized host measurements. Public resources like iHMP were specifically designed around longitudinal multi-omic host–microbiome integration, but even these are observational rather than universal intervention simulators.

Below are pivots I would consider for an ISEF-level computational project. Ranking is based on:

Public data availability

True subject/source independence

Free-first compute feasibility

Ability to define a fair benchmark

Probability of producing a novel biological insight rather than another classifier

1. Microbiome “state transition” model: predict recovery trajectories after perturbation
Core idea

Stop trying to build a universal digital twin. Build a perturbation-response atlas.

Question:

Given microbiome state before a perturbation, can we predict the ecological trajectory class afterward?

Examples:

antibiotic exposure → recovery

dietary change → stabilization

fecal microbiota transplantation → restoration

disease flare → remission

The model predicts:

recovery speed

taxa/function trajectory

resilience vs collapse phenotype

Falsifiable hypothesis

A model trained only on pre-perturbation microbiome states can predict post-perturbation recovery trajectories better than baseline ecological similarity methods.

Benchmark

Train:

leave-one-subject-out

leave-one-study-out

Compare against:

persistence baseline (“tomorrow = today”)

nearest-neighbor microbiome similarity

generalized linear model

random forest/XGBoost

simple autoregression

Win condition:

A predefined improvement on unseen subjects/studies.

Intervention labels	YES
Longitudinal samples	YES
Absolute abundance	Helpful but not mandatory
External validation	YES
Permissions	Usually no for public processed data

Potential datasets:

antibiotic perturbation cohorts

FMT cohorts

iHMP longitudinal studies

Core idea

Do not predict disease.

The contribution is the representation, not a medical classifier.

Possible tasks:

sample age prediction

subject identity recovery

temporal ordering

functional pathway reconstruction

cross-study batch robustness

A self-supervised microbiome representation trained without labels transfers better to unseen cohorts than taxonomic abundance PCA/CLR embeddings.

3. Absolute-abundance ecological twin: separate “relative illusion” from true dynamics
Core idea

A major microbiome problem:

Most sequencing data are compositional.

A bacterium appearing to increase may simply be because another taxon decreased.

Build a twin that asks:

Do absolute microbial loads improve prediction of ecosystem transitions compared with relative abundance alone?

Hypothesis

Absolute abundance-aware models predict longitudinal microbiome changes more accurately than relative abundance-only models.

Model comparison

Version A:

relative abundance

Version B:

qPCR-adjusted abundance

Version C:

inferred absolute abundance

Prediction:

future community state.

Needs
Requirement	Status
Intervention labels	No
Absolute abundance	YES
Paired qPCR	YES
External validation	YES
Challenge

Absolute abundance datasets are limited.

Some recent digital-twin style work emphasizes this limitation: simulations based only on relative abundance remain projections rather than fully validated dynamic twins.

5. Mechanistic digital twin: infer ecological interaction networks and test stability
Core idea

Avoid predicting exact abundance.

Predict ecological rules.

Question:

Which microbial interactions explain stable vs unstable community states?

Model:

generalized Lotka–Volterra

neural ODE

sparse causal graph

Output:

interaction network.

Test:

Can inferred networks predict future perturbation response?

Hypothesis

Interaction-aware models generalize better across individuals than correlation-based networks.

What I would explicitly avoid
❌ “Improve AUROC on disease prediction”

Usually fails because:

cohort leakage

batch effects

geography dominates biology

❌ “Digital twin simulator” without interventions

A model that predicts held-out observations is not necessarily a twin.

❌ “Novel microbial signatures”

Unless independently validated, this becomes:

discovered after looking at the test set.

❌ Changing thresholds after seeing failures

The benchmark must be frozen before evaluation.

If the goal is a genuinely defensible ISEF project

I would choose:

“A perturbation-response microbiome resilience model: predicting ecosystem recovery after controlled microbial disruption.”

The scientific claim becomes narrow:

“We discovered transferable features of microbiome resilience.”

not:

“We built a human microbiome digital twin.”

The first is testable with public data. The second currently demands experimental intervention, dense longitudinal sampling, and validation infrastructure that public-only projects rarely have.
