# Meta2DB composite shotgun source lead - not 84 qualified test studies

Live records read 2026-09-27: Zenodo v1 https://zenodo.org/records/17315984 ; original methods https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.03.616398v2.full-text ; published article listing https://www.ovid.com/journals/bioinf/fulltext/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag422~meta2db-curated-shotgun-metagenomic-feature-sets-and . Zenodo reports 13,897 samples from 84 studies, 23 disease states and 34 geographies. Files listed there: 51.9 MB metadata CSV (published MD5 be523d58a63947765d728f1f4f89b07a), 420.9 MB profile zip (MD5 da6a26abecf2a951fbc7402fe8919e26), 46.1 MB lineage zip, 86.0 kB data definitions PDF and 40.7 kB metadata-field definitions spreadsheet. No file was downloaded or outcome scored in this screen.

The methods describe deliberate selection for Illumina WGS, public raw data/metadata, defined disease and a binary diseased/control label. They explicitly reuse some studies from curatedMetagenomicData and the iHMP disease repository. This is a harmonized composite with potentially duplicated source families, not 84 independent new cohorts for this digital-twin task. Disease classification is not subject trajectory, drug perturbation, species removal or causal intervention forecasting. Neither sample count nor disease label establishes repeated biological subjects, per-study units, patient baseline, treatment windows or same-task comparator eligibility. Study/source crosswalk, assay definitions, original permissions, license of deposited derived files and exact MGnify/other-candidate overlap require checks before reuse or leaderboard counting. The preprint's CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 refers to the preprint; do not silently apply it to the data. This is a breadth and metadata-workflow lead only, with zero admitted datasets/benchmark results.

Follow-up API/field-dictionary check on 2026-09-27: the live Zenodo record JSON at https://zenodo.org/api/records/17315984 explicitly labels the *deposit* `cc-by-4.0` and lists exact file checksums/URLs. This differs from the bioRxiv preprint's CC BY-NC-ND license; cite the deposit license only for its own deposited files, and still verify source-study use restrictions and human-data scope before redistribution. The 40,706-byte `meta2db_metadata_field_definitions.xlsx` was downloaded solely for outcome-blind dictionary inspection and matched its published MD5 `e84e51a92305cd8e1523c6cb9f8a5924`; it is not committed. Those definitions establish *possible* columns, not their completeness or trustworthy subject/time/exposure linkage in any actual study. No 51.9-MB metadata rows or 420.9-MB abundance profiles were downloaded, and no study was admitted.

The pinned metadata CSV was downloaded and MD5-checked for an outcome-blind aggregate screen on 2026-09-27. It is Latin-1 encoded, 13,897 rows by 171 fields. `results/meta2db_metadata_screen.json` and `scripts/meta2db_metadata_screen.py` reproduce aggregate counts, with no row-level content checked in. There are 84 project_name labels but 197 project_id labels. All rows have a nominal host_subject_id; 807 project-name/subject label combinations recur (4,135 rows) across 22 project names, but this alone does not establish distinct people or independent longitudinal units. Only 1,246 rows across 9 named projects have populated `days_from_first_collection`, and only 262 project-name/subject label combinations repeat among these time-populated rows. The antibiotic-use field is populated on 7,401 rows, 472 marked TRUE; antibiotic-family data occur on just 77 rows. These fields are not course start/end or control labels. An explicit known mirror is `2022_American_Gut_Project`: 648 rows all under PRJEB11419, already present in the old MGnify audit at project level. No abundance profile was fetched, no score fitted, no study admitted. This is source metadata, not a benchmark.

Longitudinal field triage, 2026-09-27: the pinned aggregate report now lists nine project-name groups with any `days_from_first_collection` values. `2015_BackhedF`/PRJEB6456 has 269 time-populated rows among 92 subject labels, 91 with varying day and 23 repeated labels with changing antibiotic-use flag; original first-year infant/mother article https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S1931-3128(15)00162-6 concerns gut maturation, delivery and feeding. Neither a changing yes/no antibiotic metadata flag nor infant age establishes a timed antibiotic course, counterfactual or recovery result. `2016_VincentC`/PRJNA297252 has 170 time-populated rows and 50 repeatedly observed labels marked antibiotics TRUE, with no changing flag in those rows. `2017_HallAB`/PRJNA385949 has 236 time rows and is an old-audit project-level mirror, with antibiotic field unavailable. More detailed per-project aggregate counts are in the result JSON. These are leads for source-specific methods/rights/time validation, not admitted cohorts, intervention results or clinical advice.

Original-paper check on the Backhed lead, 2026-09-27: the Cell source https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S1931-3128(15)00162-6 sampled 98 mothers and infants at birth, four and twelve months. It reports five infants with antibiotic treatment before four months and Table 1 reports 24.5% antibiotic use from months 4-12. The coarse three-visit schedule, maturation, feeding and delivery effects make the Meta2DB count of 23 repeated labels with changing antibiotic flag unfit as a count of treatment courses or recovery cases. No per-infant onset/stop schedule or fair causal comparator established.

Original VincentC paper check, 2026-09-27: https://microbiomejournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40168-016-0156-3 reports 98 hospitalized patients/229 stool samples and 89% received perioperative cefazolin. Analyses considered binary medication exposure 1-3 days before stool collection, excluded eight CDI/colonization cases, and adjusted for overlapping drugs and hospitalization time. The Meta2DB subset's 50 repeated subject labels marked antibiotics TRUE do not represent 50 isolated antibiotic courses, known treatment end dates, or a recovery validation sample. Laxatives, age, hospital conditions and multiple drugs are potential confounders. No profile or outcome opened for this screen.
