# Chemical-panel admission implementation, 7 October 2026

A new offline preprocessing gate separates complete metadata from analyte-specific measured eligibility. It consumes only explicit observation flags, not chemical values. Source-specific subject keys prevent merging the same label across datasets. Duplicate specimens and technical-time coordinates are rejected. Conflicting supplement arms or non-ordered before/during specimens produce abstention. Each requested analyte must meet the frozen per-phase specimen minimum; an additional metric counts subjects with sufficient fully complete same-specimen panels. Units must be explicitly verified, but the caller still owns the provenance of that evidence.

The distinction is substantive: a subject can have enough observed samples for each analyte without enough specimens where all analytes were jointly observed. A synthetic fixture establishes this case. Another fixture shows that loss of one analyte does not erase the eligible count for a different analyte, while joint-panel eligibility correctly fails. Other negative fixtures cover leakage, unknown phase, raw values, nonboolean flags, duplicate specimen/time, arm conflict and unverified units. Numeric non-finite/sentinel handling itself belongs to the preceding source-aware parser, not this flag-only function.

Sixteen new offline tests pass. Full suite: 467 passed, one skipped, one existing LightGBM warning, 41.62 seconds. No cohort export, model training or real chemical-value analysis was run. The resistant-starch cohort's 175-versus-174 subject discrepancy, partial chemical parseability and unresolved inclusion semantics remain open. This function does not resolve those facts or authorize a forecast. It never grants independent validation, clinical eligibility or causal inference.
