# Pre-result method check: neighborhood-trust twin (NTT), 2026-09-28 11:12 IST

Question: Can a query-assemblage-specific neighborhood correction help the local population prior without a trained deep model? This is an engineering invention candidate combining known similarity smoothing, effective-sample-size shrinkage and a baseline fallback. It is **not** yet a new algorithm in the literature sense, a causal twin, or a validated improvement over existing tools. Do not claim priority without prior-art search and untouched-source tests.

Freeze before checking result vectors: for binary assemblage query z and training assemblages Z, Jaccard similarity s_j=intersection/union. Use weights w_j=s_j^4, zero for s_j=0, and n_eff=(sum w)^2/sum(w^2). Global prior p0_i is training sample mean composition masked to query-present taxa and renormalized. Local pL_i is the w-weighted mean of training compositions, masked and renormalized. If weighted local mass on present query taxa is zero, fall back to p0. Blend coefficient a=[n_eff/(n_eff+5)]*[max_j s_j]^2 (no outcome/target abundance), and p=(1-a)p0+a pL. Reject queries with present taxa entirely absent from training; respect zeros/simplex. Plain local comparator is pL (same fallback). Use identical train folds, same input data and information for all methods.

Secondary: plain local comparator, prediction fallback counts, and alpha distribution; no p-value, top-tool verdict or post-hoc coefficient tuning. Negative outcomes stay in report. ## Prior-art and observed-result correction, 11:14 IST

A direct prior-art read after the frozen-rule diagnostic found a close published antecedent: Bairey et al., *Model-free prediction of microbiome compositions* (2024), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10832217/ . It already predicts microbial abundance from presence/absence with Jaccard kNN, compares a mean-abundance null, relates per-sample gain to neighborhood density, and discusses antibiotic perturbations. The present Jaccard-weighted local composition and effective-neighbor/maximum-similarity shrinkage is therefore an incremental heuristic, not a completely novel algorithm or the platform's lead invention. It is not equivalent to the published exact kNN baseline: the published estimator averages selected top-k neighbors with per-species normalization whereas this code uses a weighted whole-training neighbor mean and a convex prior blend. A published kNN implementation/faithful reproduction still has to be included as a same-task comparator before a method claim.

The fixed internal six-table diagnostic (`results/neighbor_trust_six_dataset_check.json`) favors the trust blend over PresenceMean on four of six conditional covered-row *dataset medians* (Drosophila Gut, Soil Vitro, Soil Vivo, Human Gut), but loses Ocean and Human Oral. The Human Gut median difference is only ~0.000006 in Bray-Curtis. Seventeen of 1,268 queries abstain for unseen-present taxa; no subject grouping or external source-family independence is proved. On Drosophila Gut and Soil Vitro the unblended local comparator is markedly better, and on several tables the trust blend appears to dilute it. This is an exploratory negative/ambiguous result, not evidence that our method beats the published tool. Do not tune the fixed coefficients on these same rows and call that a new untouched result. The lead-invention search moves elsewhere.
